# Ash's Journey — A Collector's Chronicle

*One binder to retrace an entire journey: from a boy leaving Pallet Town to the first-ever World Champion. Every Pokémon that walked a stretch of that road earns a place here — and the rarity of its card marks how far it traveled.*

---

## The Premise

Ash's journey is finished. From Kanto to the Coronation, the whole road has been walked — which means this binder has a **final, knowable roster.** Every Pokémon that will ever belong here has already earned its place; nothing new will ever be added. That is the gift of a completed story: it can be collected in full.

This is not a Pokédex to fill. It is a **journey to retrace.** A Pokémon claims a slot only if it *mattered* to the tale — caught, fought, befriended, or crossed paths with in a moment worth remembering. The ones Ash never met stay out. The ones he did are ranked not by how rare they are in the wild, but by **how deep they run in the story.**

And the binder is bound to no single set. **Any card, from any era, may fill a slot** — a vintage holo, an old EX-era pull, or the newest chase card — so long as its rarity matches the rank a Pokémon earned. (Older sets named their rarities differently; mapping them onto our ranks is a short guide of its own, noted at the end.)

## The Ranks

Every Pokémon begins at the foot of the mountain and climbs one rank for each milestone it clears. The rank *is* the story — one look at a card tells you how many chapters that Pokémon lived through.

## The Milestones

Every Pokémon here cleared the first gate. Each of the six that follow adds a token for a spot on the binder.

**The Catch** — Common / Uncommon *⚫*:
Ash caught it. That alone earns a place at the foot of the binder, even for a Pokémon that spent the whole adventure resting at Professor Oak's. A caught partner that did nothing more — a Tauros in the tall grass, a Muk at the lab — belongs right here. It walked through the door, and the door is where every journey begins.

**The Opponent** *+💫*:
Ash faced this Pokémon as an **opponent in official competition** — a League, a tournament, a Contest, the World Coronation stage. The proving grounds, where results are recorded and legends are measured.

**The Teammate** *+💫*:
It stood on Ash's side of the field and fought for him in **in official competition** — trained, battled, earned its place in the party.

**The Encounter** *+💫*:
A wild Pokémon Ash never made his own, but whose path crossed his in a moment that shaped the journey — a legendary glimpsed at the world's edge in an episode no one forgets. He didn't catch it yet left its mark all the same.This rule is mainly to give Legendaries a chasable path.

**The Bond** *+💫*:
Somewhere on the road, Ash and this Pokémon carried each other through something — a hurdle cleared, a friendship that sparked an evolution, a farewell that still aches. Ownership is beside the point here; the depth of the tie is what counts. (This is why Meowth belongs — never Ash's, yet woven into the story all the same.)

**The Glory** *+💫*:
It stood on the active roster the day Ash lifted a trophy — an official League or Championship, won and written into the record.

**The Loyal** *+💫*:
The rarest star of all: the companion who stayed. This Pokémon joined Ash in one chapter and was still at his side, earning its keep, in a **later region** — the ones who came back, who never truly left. (Charizard returning from the Charicific Valley to fight at the Battle Frontier; Pidgeot and Primeape reappearing seasons on.)

**Three lines worth keeping straight:**
- **Encounter or Bond** — a *meeting* versus a *tie*. A wild crossing of paths is an Encounter; a memorable emotional experience is a Bond. They are not rivals: one wild Pokémon can earn both, which is exactly how a never-owned companion like Meowth reaches two milestone tokens.
- **Glory** means on the roster *the day a trophy was won* — not merely "fought well."
- **Loyal** measures *staying power*, a Pokemon that earns an aforementioned milestone in a region outside their original one. It always rides alongside another star — a returning Pokémon is, by definition, earning some milestone in that later region — so it never travels alone. A loyal companion carries two milestone tokens at the very least.

## The Rules of the Chase

**Rank sets the cards** A Pokemon rank carries a spot in the binder for its preceding cards. A pokemon of Rank Ultra Rare (4💫) should have a common/uncommon (only if catched), rare, double rare and finally ultra rare card spot on the binder

**Every version feeds the same slot.** A species draws its stars from *all* its appearances in the journey — Ash's own, a rival's, a wild one. Pikachu's slot is fed by Ash's partner (caught, teammate, bonded, champion, loyal), by the wild Pikachu of a famous goodbye (encounter), and by any rival's Pikachu faced in a League (battle tested). One slot, every thread of the tale woven in.

**The journey carries forward.** When a Pokémon earns a star, that triumph travels **up its evolution line** to every later form. Charmander's rescue-in-the-storm bond rides with it into Charmeleon, and on into Charizard. So each card is a snapshot of the journey *so far* at that stage, and the final form carries the whole story. The climb can level off; it never slides back.

**A goodbye is a summit.** The Pokémon Ash released or left behind — Butterfree to the winds, Lapras to its pod, Pidgeot to guard a flock — reached the *height* of their bond in that farewell. Letting go is one of the journey's highest notes, never a demotion. (A companion that left and never returned simply earns no Loyal star — staying is a badge of its own.)

## The Outfitter's Note — which cards you can actually chase

Because any card from any era qualifies, the hunt is wide open — but two things are worth carrying with you. First, older sets named their rarities differently, and a few of our ranks (Double Rare, the Illustration tiers) never existed under those names, so translating an EX-era *ex*, a *GX*, a *V*, or an old gold *Secret Rare* onto our ranks takes a short map. Second, a card must match the rank a Pokémon *earned*: a Pokémon that climbed to four stars needs a four-star-rank card, so even a beloved vintage holo won't fit a slot its Pokémon out-climbed. Drawing that translation map is the next tool whenever you want it.

## The Trail Ahead

The rules are set. What remains is to **walk the whole journey and place every Pokémon** — now across *all* regions, Kanto to Galar, not the first 151 alone:
- **Chart the full catch list**, region by region. (Kanto and the Journeys returns are done; Johto through Alola await.)
- **Pin the trophies** that fix the Glory roster — the headline wins being the Orange League, the Alola League, and the World Championship itself.
- **Pin the returns** that fix the Loyal roster — the most memorable returns made by old friends.
- **Settle Team Rocket** — how Jessie, James, and the gang's recurring Pokémon earn their place.
- **Settle the Encounters** — memorable wild encounters (mainly legendaries).
- **Draw the rarity-translation map**, so a card from any era can find its slot.

*The journey is written. Now we collect it.*
